Our Objective

Ghana's next generation of innovators, technologists, and problem-solvers will not all emerge from urban centres. Spursbase Foundation's Innovation & Technology Programme ensures that children in rural and underserved communities have access to the digital skills, creative tools, and innovation opportunities they need to shape resilient futures.

From hands-on ICT exposure for girls to robotics exploration for rural students, our programme sparks curiosity, builds confidence, and opens doors to careers and possibilities that once felt out of reach.

The Challenge

For children growing up in rural Ghana, the digital divide is not just about access to devices, it is about the absence of role models, opportunities, and environments that make technology feel possible and relevant to their lives.

The Digital Divide

Rural students have limited exposure to digital tools, careers, and innovation ecosystems. Without hands-on experience, technology remains abstract, and futures in ICT, engineering, and innovation remain invisible possibilities.

Girls Locked Out

Gender norms and limited role models keep girls away from technology and STEM pathways. At the secondary level, the gender gap in ICT and science subjects remains significant, limiting the pipeline of female innovators and technologists.

Untapped Rural Talent

Rural communities hold vast, untapped potential. Children like Dzidefo dream of building robots and solving community problems, but without structured opportunity, those dreams rarely become reality.

Innovation Without Limits

At the heart of our Innovation & Technology Programme are the young people whose lives it transforms. These are their stories.

Dzidefo, The Robotics Dreamer

Dzidefo, 16, grew up in a rural community where opportunities to explore technology felt out of reach. He dreamed of being a lawyer, a journalist, and secretly, a tech innovator building robots. When he joined the Rural Community Innovation Challenge, everything changed. He learned creative thinking, problem-solving, and innovation, and for the first time, his ideas had a platform. Today, Dzidefo dreams of building a robot he can control with his phone, and he is determined to turn his ideas into real solutions that benefit his community and inspire other young innovators across rural Ghana.

650 Girls at Awudome SHS

At Awudome Senior High School, curiosity filled the room as 650 girls encountered technology not as observers, but as future creators. For many, it was the first time seeing digital tools as pathways to careers, leadership, and problem-solving. Questions flowed, confidence grew, and aspirations took shape in a single afternoon.

Our Approach

We bring innovation and technology directly to rural children, through hands-on experiences, community-relevant challenges, and digital exposure that makes technology feel attainable and exciting.

Learn by Doing

Our programmes are built on hands-on learning, students build, create, test, and iterate. Innovation is not taught as a concept; it is experienced as a practice, grounded in real community challenges.

Girls-First Digital Access

We actively design opportunities that centre girls' access to ICT, from Girls in ICT Day to digital skills integration within our Elevate Hub, ensuring that the next generation of female innovators in Ghana has a strong foundation.

Community-Relevant Innovation

We challenge students to look beyond the classroom and into their own communities. By connecting innovation to real local problems, we make technology relevant, purposeful, and motivating for rural learners.

Educator Engagement

Our Teacher Engagement Programme brings headteachers and educators together, sharing strategies, tools, and best practices that strengthen their capacity to support innovation and digital learning within their schools.
